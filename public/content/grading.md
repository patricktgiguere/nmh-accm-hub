# How You Are Graded on Our Rotations

You are assessed against the ACGME Critical Care Anesthesiology Milestones (version 2.1, implemented July 2022), which cover the six core competencies, each broken into subcompetencies. Two tools track your progress.

- **Milestone evaluations.** Through the year, faculty complete fuller milestone evaluations. These are reviewed with you in your individual meetings and by the Clinical Competency Committee. The subcompetency descriptions below are what those evaluations look for.
- **Weekly myTIPreport evaluation.** A short check on your progress each ICU week, with specific, actionable feedback on what to keep doing and what to work on next.

The descriptions below explain, in plain language, what meeting and exceeding expectations look like on our rotations, with examples from daytime rounding weeks in the CTICU. Level 4, ready for independent practice, is our program's target in every subcompetency by graduation. Level 5 is exceeding expectations, and few fellows reach it in every area.

## The weekly form's scale

The weekly form uses five levels aligned to the milestones.

- **Level 1, requires direct guidance.** You are learning the basics and need an attending alongside you.
- **Level 2, requires frequent guidance.** You handle routine problems but need regular help with plans and decisions.
- **Level 3, requires occasional guidance.** You run most of the day yourself and check in on complex or high-stakes decisions.
- **Level 4, ready for independent practice.** You manage the full range of patients and problems on your own.
- **Level 5, aspirational role model.** You go above and beyond and others learn from you.

Scores of 2 to 3 early in the year are on track. What we look for is steady movement toward Level 4.

Subcompetencies marked *(weekly form)* are areas your attending also comments on weekly.

## Patient Care

### PC1 Patient assessment and care plan *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You keep reassessing your patients through the day, catch the presentation that does not fit, and adjust the plan for complex patients, such as a post-cardiotomy patient whose course is drifting from the expected recovery.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** Colleagues come to you about unusual presentations, and you help build or revise unit pathways.

### PC2 Crisis management *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You see deterioration coming before it happens and lead the bedside team through it, from early bleeding or tamponade after surgery to a sudden device alarm.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You manage resources beyond the bedside during a crisis, such as calling in the OR team, perfusion, or extra staff, and moving resources across the unit.

### PC3 Procedures, technical skills, and interpretation
- **Meets (Level 4):** You perform common and advanced ICU procedures proficiently, manage their complications, and recognize unusual findings on imaging, echo, and monitoring.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** Others consult you on difficult procedures, and you help bring new evidence-based procedures into the unit.

### PC4 Respiratory failure and ventilation
- **Meets (Level 4):** You manage ventilation independently and recognize when a patient needs advanced therapy such as prone positioning, inhaled pulmonary vasodilators, or ECMO.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You adapt advanced ventilation strategies on your own and help shape respiratory care protocols and policy.

### PC5 Shock and organ dysfunction *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You combine advanced data, such as echo, PA catheter, and device parameters, into one coherent plan for shock, and you act early to limit organ injury and its long-term effects, for example in vasoplegia or RV failure after cardiac surgery.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You are a go-to resource for complex shock and multiorgan failure, and you help develop diagnostic or management algorithms.

## Medical Knowledge

### MK1 Pharmacology
- **Meets (Level 4):** You choose and adjust drugs with interactions, organ dysfunction, ECMO, and CRRT in mind, not just standard doses.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** Colleagues consult you on pharmacotherapy for complex patients.

### MK2 Pathophysiology of critical illness
- **Meets (Level 4):** You explain the physiology behind your patients' problems well enough that the team uses you as a resource.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You build and prioritize differentials in the most complex patients and anticipate complications before they appear.

## Systems-Based Practice

### SBP1 Patient safety and quality improvement
- **Meets (Level 4):** You can lead a safety event review, take part in disclosure to a family, and design and carry out a QI project.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You engage teams to change systems, mentor others through disclosure, and lead QI work beyond the unit.

### SBP2 Care coordination and handoffs
- **Meets (Level 4):** Your handoffs and transitions are a model for others, including OR to ICU handoffs, transfers out, and coordination with consultants.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You find gaps in how care is coordinated and work to fix the process.

### SBP3 Your role in the health system
- **Meets (Level 4):** You use the system efficiently and choose cost-conscious care without compromising patients.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You lead or advocate for system changes that improve the value of care.

## Practice-Based Learning and Improvement

### PBLI1 Evidence-based practice *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You appraise and apply evidence even when it is weak or conflicting, and you can explain your choice on rounds.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You teach others how to appraise evidence, for example at journal club, or help develop guidelines.

### PBLI2 Reflective practice and growth *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You ask for feedback consistently, act on it, and adjust your own learning plan.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You model reflective practice and help residents and students build their own learning plans.

## Professionalism

### PROF1 Professional behavior and ethics *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You stay professional under stress, head off your own lapses, seek help early with difficult interpersonal situations, and use ethics resources when dilemmas arise.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You coach others whose behavior falls short and address the system issues behind ethical problems.

### PROF2 Accountability *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You keep your work complete and on time even on busy, high-acuity days, with your own strategies to stay ahead.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You help build team or unit processes that make timely, reliable work easier for everyone.

### PROF3 Well-being
- **Meets (Level 4):** You have a plan for your own well-being and understand how the work environment affects you and your colleagues. This is not a rating of how you are doing personally.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You help shape programs that address the system causes of burnout.

## Interpersonal and Communication Skills

### ICS1 Patient and family communication
- **Meets (Level 4):** You lead difficult family meetings, use shared decision making, and manage conflict between families and the team.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You mentor others in difficult conversations and conflict resolution.

### ICS2 Communication around serious illness *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You tailor how you talk about prognosis and goals of care to the illness and the family's emotions, and you lead shared decisions in high uncertainty, such as continuing or withdrawing mechanical support.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You coach others in prognostic communication and shared decision making.

### ICS3 Team communication *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** You bring together recommendations from surgery, cardiology, nursing, perfusion, and other teams into one plan, keep communication clear during a crisis, and give constructive feedback, including to people senior to you.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You model communication that values every team member's input and lead regular team feedback.

### ICS4 Communication within the health system *(weekly form)*
- **Meets (Level 4):** Your notes and verbal communication are a model for others, and you raise system problems through the right channels with ideas to fix them.
- **Exceeds (Level 5):** You help shape communication practices or policy for the unit or department.

## Weekly evaluations (myTIPreport)

- For each ICU week, a request goes automatically to your team attending, dated that Friday, based on the ACCM call schedule.
- The attending rates you in seven areas on a 1 to 5 milestone-aligned scale, plus one thing to keep doing and one thing to work on next.
- The graduation target is Level 4, ready for independent practice. Scores of 2 to 3 early in the year are on track.
- If your schedule changes, request the evaluation yourself in myTIPreport.
